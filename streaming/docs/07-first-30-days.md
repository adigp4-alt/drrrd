# 7. The first 30 days

The software was the easy part. This is the honest version of the rest.

## Set your expectations correctly

Your first stream will have between zero and three viewers, and one of them will
be a bot. This is not a sign of anything. Every large channel has a first stream
that looked exactly like that.

The realistic shape of the first month: a handful of viewers, most of whom are
passing through, and by week four one or two regulars who show up because you
were there when you said you would be. **That's the win condition for month
one** — not follower count, not a viral clip. One person who comes back.

## The one thing that matters most

**A schedule you can actually keep.**

Two fixed days a week, same time, for a month, beats streaming daily for two
weeks and then vanishing. Discovery on every platform rewards consistency, but
more importantly, humans do: nobody can become a regular at something that
happens at random.

Pick two slots. Put them on your channel. Put them in a calendar. Treat a
scheduled stream like a shift.

## What to actually do on stream

- **Talk constantly.** Narrate what you're thinking, what you're about to try,
  why the last thing failed. Streamers who feel "boring" are almost always just
  quiet. This is a skill and it develops in weeks, not years.
- **Play what you enjoy**, but check the category size. A brand-new streamer in
  a category with 40,000 other channels is invisible; the same person in a
  category with 200 is browsable. Smaller, active categories are the single
  biggest discovery lever you have.
- **Have an opinion.** People follow a person, not a gameplay feed. The
  commentary is the product; the game is the backdrop.
- **Assume nobody is watching, act like someone is.** Someone arrives at minute
  50 and hears only what you're saying then.

## Growth, in order of what actually works

1. **Clips and shorts.** Cut 30–60 seconds out of every stream with LosslessCut,
   post them vertically to TikTok/Shorts/Reels. This is where new viewers
   genuinely come from now. One clip per stream, minimum.
2. **Be a viewer in other people's chats.** Not spamming your link — actually
   participating in three or four small channels you like. Streamers who
   discover each other raid each other, and a raid is 20 people arriving at once.
3. **Raid at the end of every stream.** Costs nothing, makes friends.
4. **A Discord**, once you have three or four regulars. Not before — an empty
   server looks worse than no server.
5. **Post when you go live**, in one place, consistently.

What does *not* work: follow-for-follow, dropping your link in strangers' chats,
buying anything, and posting "streaming now!" into the void with no clip
attached.

## A sensible month-one plan

**Week 1** — Two streams, 90 minutes each. Goal: nothing breaks, and you get
used to talking to an empty room. Watch 10 minutes of each recording back.

**Week 2** — Two streams, 2 hours. Add one clip per stream posted somewhere.
Start greeting anyone who says anything by name.

**Week 3** — Two streams. Sit in two other small streamers' chats during the
week. Raid someone at the end of each of yours.

**Week 4** — Two streams. Look at what your best clip was and why. Decide
whether your category is too crowded. Adjust one thing, not five.

At the end: you have eight streams of practice, eight clips out in the world,
and you know whether you enjoy this. That last one is the real output of month
one.

## Protecting yourself, longer term

- **Don't stream tired or upset.** It's a live public performance and it will
  show up in your mood as much as your voice.
- **Set an end time before you start**, and honour it. "Just one more game" at
  2am is how burnout arrives in month three.
- **Take the numbers off the screen.** Hide your viewer count while live if you
  can. Watching it tick between 2 and 3 for an hour is corrosive and tells you
  nothing.
- **Two streams a week you keep for six months** beats five a week you keep for
  three. Aim to still be doing this in a year.

## When to change something

Give any change four weeks before judging it. If after two months you have no
regulars at all, the usual causes, in order:

1. Inconsistent schedule
2. Category far too crowded
3. Not talking enough
4. Nothing being clipped or posted anywhere else

It is almost never the overlay, the camera, or the microphone.
